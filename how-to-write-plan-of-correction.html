How to Write a Plan of Correction (POC)

Operational contributor

Christopher Weedman, founder of Evo Engineering and a former Texas-licensed nursing facility administrator (2010–2024). Regulatory sources checked October 26, 2026.

A Plan of Correction (POC) is the facility's written response to deficiencies cited on a CMS-2567 or state survey document. This guide follows the five corrective elements described in the official Texas HHSC nursing-facility POC training, which cites the State Operations Manual Chapter 7. CMS publishes current Chapter 7 material through its Nursing Home Enforcement page. Facilities should confirm the current instructions and deadlines that apply to their survey and jurisdiction.

A facility may separately dispute a citation, but its POC explains how the cited issue will be corrected, how other potentially affected residents will be identified, what systemic measures will prevent recurrence, how compliance will be monitored, and when corrective actions will be completed. Submitting and implementing a POC does not by itself mean the facility agrees with the citation.

1. Correct the Deficient Practice for Affected Residents

Describe what actions were taken to correct the cited deficiency. This section focuses on correcting the specific issue identified during survey.

Examples may include:

  • Repairs
  • Staff education
  • Documentation correction
  • Resident intervention
  • Policy implementation

2. Identify Residents Potentially Affected

Describe how the facility will identify other residents who could be affected by the same deficient practice and what evaluation or corrective action will follow.

3. Make Systemic Changes

Describe the measures or system changes the facility will implement to prevent the deficient practice from recurring.

Examples may include:

  • Policy or procedure changes
  • Education or competency validation
  • Workflow or documentation changes
  • Equipment or environmental correction
  • Defined escalation and follow-up

4. Establish Monitoring and Responsibility

Describe how the facility will monitor performance to confirm the corrective action is sustained, and identify the position responsible for that monitoring.

Common monitoring methods include:

  • Audits
  • Department reviews
  • Environmental rounds
  • Record reviews
  • QAPI oversight

5. Establish Completion Dates

State the date by which corrective action will be completed, consistent with the applicable instructions and implementation timeline.

Hypothetical Composite POC Structure

This educational example is a composite, not an actual facility response or a guarantee that a regulator will accept similar language.

Deficiency: Survey identified incomplete medication storage checks on two medication carts.
Correction: The carts were reviewed, expired items were removed, and licensed nursing staff were re-educated on medication storage expectations.
Residents Potentially Affected: Residents receiving medications from the affected carts were identified as potentially affected. Additional medication carts were reviewed for similar concerns.
Systemic Measures: The facility revised its medication-cart check process, assigned routine checks by position, and established leadership review of missed or incomplete checks.
Responsible Party: Director of Nursing or designee.
Monitoring: Medication cart audits will be completed three times weekly for four weeks, then reviewed through QAPI.
Completion Date: Corrective actions completed by the stated POC completion date.
