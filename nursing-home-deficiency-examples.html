Nursing Home Deficiency Examples

Nursing home deficiencies are citations issued when a facility fails to meet federal requirements under 42 CFR Part 483. Deficiencies are assigned a scope and severity level based on resident impact and the extent of the issue.

Understanding common survey deficiencies helps facilities prepare corrective actions, reduce recurring risk, and strengthen compliance programs.

Medication Error (F761)

Deficiency: Survey identified that medications were not stored or handled in accordance with facility policy and accepted standards.
Potential Cause: Medication cart checks were inconsistent, expired medications were not removed timely, or staff were unclear on storage expectations.
Typical Correction: Remove affected medications, re-educate licensed nursing staff, review medication storage policy, and verify carts or medication rooms are compliant.
Monitoring: DON or designee completes medication storage audits several times weekly, trends findings, and reports results through QAPI.

Infection Prevention and Control (F880)

Deficiency: Survey observed infection prevention practices that did not follow facility policy or standard precautions.
Potential Cause: Staff may have missed hand hygiene, used PPE inconsistently, or failed to follow isolation signage and supply expectations.
Typical Correction: Provide immediate staff education, correct supplies or signage, reinforce infection prevention policy, and validate staff competency where needed.
Monitoring: Infection Preventionist or designee conducts hand hygiene, PPE, and isolation practice observations, then reviews results with leadership and QAPI.

Documentation Deficiency (F842)

Deficiency: Survey found that the clinical record did not contain complete or accurate documentation to support resident care and services.
Potential Cause: Documentation expectations were not clear, follow-up notes were missed, or interdisciplinary updates were not consistently entered.
Typical Correction: Review affected records, complete appropriate late or clarifying entries when allowed by policy, educate staff on documentation requirements, and reinforce timely charting expectations.
Monitoring: Nursing leadership reviews a sample of records weekly for completeness, reports trends, and follows up with staff or departments as needed.

Understanding Scope and Severity

Deficiencies are assigned levels A through L. Surveyors evaluate resident impact and scope, including whether the issue is isolated, a pattern, or widespread. Scope and severity influence correction expectations, follow-up activity, and how urgently leadership must address the cited practice. Common Patterns in Deficiencies

Common survey findings often involve:

  • Documentation breakdowns
  • Medication administration processes
  • Infection prevention practices
  • Care planning failures
  • Inconsistent monitoring systems
