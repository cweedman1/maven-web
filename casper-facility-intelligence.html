CASPER & Facility Intelligence

Resources for translating facility performance signals into QAPI priorities, survey readiness, and operational follow-through.

Current CMS Reporting Context

System update: CMS moved MDS assessment submissions and the related Provider and Quality Measure reports from CASPER to iQIES in April 2023. The official QTSO sample iQIES MDS 3.0 reports page describes that migration and current report formats. CASPER remains a familiar legacy term and may still apply to other report areas, so teams should verify the current CMS access path for each report. Source checked October 26, 2026.

Many administrators begin QAPI preparation by reviewing current CMS facility reports. The reports are inputs—not conclusions—and can help facilities identify trends, monitor performance, and prioritize improvement work.

Reports provide information. QAPI provides action. The administrator's job is to connect the two: review the reports, identify the risk, determine why the trend is occurring, and make sure the facility has a practical plan to address it.

What Current CMS Reports Provide

MDS and other CMS reporting systems organize information already reported to CMS. Administrators use current reports to understand where the facility is performing well, where it may be drifting, and where closer operational review is warranted.

Common review areas include:

  • Facility Quality Measures
  • Five-Star performance
  • Long-stay measures
  • Short-stay measures
  • Survey history
  • Deficiency history
  • Rehospitalization trends
  • Antipsychotic measures
  • Pressure injury measures
  • Infection-related indicators

Medicare Care Compare

Many administrators review Medicare Care Compare alongside internal CMS reports because it shows how facility performance appears publicly. Care Compare presents Five-Star ratings, health inspections, staffing, Quality Measures, public survey history, and facility comparisons in a public-facing format.

For an administrator, the point is not cosmetic. Care Compare helps leadership understand how survey history, staffing data, and quality measures may be interpreted outside the building. If Care Compare shows movement in the wrong direction, the facility should understand why and decide whether the issue belongs in QAPI, survey readiness, department monitoring, or leadership follow-up.

What Administrators Review

A useful report review is not just reading numbers off a page. Administrators look at Quality Measures, Five-Star trends, long-stay metrics, short-stay metrics, repeat deficiencies, and survey history to see whether performance is improving, declining, or staying unchanged.

The trend direction matters. A measure that is still acceptable but moving the wrong way may deserve attention before it becomes a survey issue. A repeat deficiency may show that the facility corrected the immediate problem but did not fix the process. A short-stay measure may point to discharge, therapy, nursing, or hospital-transfer patterns that need a closer look.

In practice, the administrator is usually looking for priorities, not explanations from the report alone. If a measure changed, the next step is to compare it against what the building already knows: recent incidents, department audits, staffing changes, resident acuity, MDS coding patterns, infection logs, hospitalization reviews, and prior survey findings.

Common Warning Signs

Facilities should not wait for survey findings before addressing known risks. Common warning signs include:

  • Falls trending upward
  • Rehospitalizations increasing
  • Infection rates increasing
  • Antipsychotic use increasing
  • Weight loss concerns
  • Documentation issues
  • Repeat survey findings
  • Staffing instability

A warning sign does not automatically mean the facility has failed. It means leadership should ask better questions before the issue grows. Is the problem isolated to one unit? Is it tied to one shift? Did the measure change after a census change, staffing change, or process change? Is the same concern showing up in grievances, incident investigations, and audits?

From Facility Reports to QAPI

Facility reports can identify signals. QAPI creates action plans. Monitoring evaluates results. A practical workflow looks like this:

  • Current reports: Review measures, survey history, and facility trends through the applicable CMS system.
  • Identify Risk: Decide which trends or measures need leadership attention.
  • Root Cause Analysis: Determine why the issue is occurring instead of assuming the first explanation is correct.
  • QAPI Initiative: Assign ownership, define the goal, and identify what will be measured.
  • Monitoring: Track whether the intervention is working through audits, observations, reviews, or follow-up data.
  • Closure: Document the outcome, close the initiative when appropriate, or extend it with a clear reason.

Facility Intelligence

Facility intelligence is broader than any single CMS report system. Administrators also need Medicare Care Compare information, survey findings, incident investigations, staffing observations, infection prevention data, resident concerns, and operational audits.

The goal is creating a complete picture of facility performance. A single report rarely tells the whole story. When current report data, investigation findings, audit results, and department observations point in the same direction, leadership has a stronger basis for action.

This is where QAPI becomes useful. A facility can use a report to identify a signal, but it needs facility intelligence to understand whether the signal reflects a real operational issue. Strong operators use current reports as part of a routine review cycle alongside day-to-day information coming from the floor.

Strong leaders compare multiple sources before reaching conclusions. A fall trend may show up in a facility report, but the cause may be clearer in fall investigations, care plan audits, therapy notes, staffing observations, and unit-level monitoring. An infection concern may appear in quality data, but the operational explanation may be in isolation audits, supply availability, staff competency checks, and infection prevention rounds.

Example Workflow

A current facility report shows increased falls. The Administrator and DON review fall reports, care plans, investigation findings, staffing patterns, and recent monitoring. The review shows that interventions were identified after falls, but follow-through was inconsistent across shifts.

The facility completes root cause analysis and identifies inconsistent intervention communication as the main issue. A QAPI initiative is opened. The team updates the handoff process, re-educates nurses and CNAs, assigns monitoring to nursing leadership, and reviews results for 60 days. If falls decrease and intervention follow-through improves, the initiative is documented and closed. If results do not improve, leadership documents the barrier and adjusts the plan.

Common Mistakes

  • Reviewing reports without taking action
  • Tracking too many priorities at once
  • Ignoring trend direction
  • Treating data sources separately
  • Failing to connect investigations to QAPI
  • Failing to close initiatives

One of the most common mistakes is discussing falls, infections, deficiencies, staffing concerns, and report data separately instead of evaluating whether they reflect the same operational problem. That approach makes it easy to miss patterns. A stronger process brings related signals together and asks whether they point to the same underlying issue.
